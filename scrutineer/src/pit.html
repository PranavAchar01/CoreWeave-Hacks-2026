SCRUTINEER pit board — the car, on your desk, while the loop runs behind it the broadcast ↗

WHAT IT IS

A card that stays on top, with a toy car lapping the season's circuit. The car is the agent's harness as it stands right now — wings for retrieval, floor for verification, compound for sampling. Where it is on the lap is how far the current run has got; the gold line on the map in the corner is the same thing. It crosses the line when the loop decides the run, and a kept change rebuilds the car right there.

Click or hover the card for the board: runs decided, changes kept, the held-out score and what it has gained, the ten components and their levels. For the whole story, the board links to the telemetry.

KEEP IT IN VIEW

Pop it out. The ↗ on the strip gives it a window of its own that floats above every other app — switch tabs, switch apps, the car keeps turning. Chrome and Edge do this today; elsewhere the card stays on the page.

Drag it. Take the strip and put the card anywhere. It remembers where you left it.

Embed it. One line puts it on any page of yours:

<script src="https://scrutineer-demo.vercel.app/pit.js" async></script>

WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM

The strip's dot says which. LIVE means a scrutineer watch server answered, and every lap, score and verdict on the board is the loop's own, as it happens on that machine. The key never leaves it.

REPLAY means no loop answered, so the board replays the recorded season shipped with this page, one generation every 24 seconds, round again when it ends. The numbers are the season's; only the clock is the board's.

This page, on https, is always a replay: a browser will not let an https page talk to a plain http server on your machine, and the loop speaks plain http on your own hardware. So the live board is the one the loop serves you itself.

RUN IT LIVE

cd scrutineer/loop
export ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-ant-…
uv run scrutineer watch

Then open http://127.0.0.1:7777/pit — same card, green dot, real numbers. The embed does the same on any http page of yours on that machine: it finds the loop on 127.0.0.1:7777 by itself.
